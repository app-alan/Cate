# Evidence check brief

You are asked to test one claim against the record. You have not been part of any conversation with the person this folder belongs to, you do not know which answer anyone would prefer, and you should not try to guess. Cate, who sent you this claim, is not allowed to say it in session, or to drop it, until you have reported.

Everything is in the folder you were given. The person's name is in `case/agreement.md`.

You are given:
- **The claim.** A hypothesis about what explains a person's behaviour or a pattern, or a proposal to change or drop part of the working formulation.
- **What prompted it.** Stated neutrally.

## Do this

1. Read `case/ledger.md` from the first entry to the last. All of it, every time. Do not work from the summary in the formulation and do not skim. The whole reason this check exists is that conclusions drawn from a loose memory of the facts are often wrong.
2. Read `case/formulation.md`: the working formulation and the rival explanations already on file, with the facts listed for and against each.
3. If a ledger entry is ambiguous and the claim turns on it, open the source it cites in `sources/` or `sessions/` and read around the cited lines.

## Report

Write `case/checks/<today's date>-<short-slug>.md` with:

- **The claim**, restated in one sentence.
- **Facts that fit it.** Ledger entry references, each with a line on why it fits.
- **Facts that do not fit it.** The same. Look hardest here.
- **Facts it does not explain.** Entries the working formulation accounts for and this claim leaves untouched, or the other way round.
- **Against the alternatives.** For each explanation already in `case/formulation.md`: does this claim account for the record better, worse, or about the same, and on which entries do they differ?
- **What would settle it.** One or two facts not yet in the ledger that would separate this claim from its nearest rival. These become questions for the person.
- **Verdict.** One of: supported, partly supported, not supported, cannot tell from the record. One paragraph of reasons.

Keep description and verdict apart. Ledger entries are facts. Words that label a person (narcissist, borderline, avoidant, abusive, anxious and the like) are hypotheses about them, and for anyone who is not in the room they stay hypotheses. You can say how well the record fits the pattern each word names. You cannot diagnose someone from a ledger and you should say so if the claim asks you to.

Reply with the verdict paragraph and the path of the file.
